# Rejected: star pips painted on paper tiles

The first star pair was painted as art *on* paper: the filled star sat on a square mulberry tile and the
empty one on a round paper disc. Two problems, both only visible once the pips were rendered at the size a
card actually draws them.

- At roughly eleven pixels the tile is the shape you read, not the star. A row of five pips read as a row of
  five pale squares. Re-cropping the tile inward helped and did not fix it: the tile was still there.
- The pair did not match each other. A square tile beside a round disc is two different objects, not one
  filled state and one empty state of the same object.

The replacement pair is the star shape alone with no backing plate: solid tarnished gold for a filled pip,
a hollow scratched-iron outline for an empty one. The card's paper identity is carried by the frame, where
it belongs, and the pips are left to do the one job they have.
